A Novel Hybrid Deep Learning Model Using U-Net and Vision Transformer for Polyp Segmentation in Medical Imaging
Prity Rani Das, et al.
Official Open-Source Research Implementation • Colonoscopy Diagnostic Vision

Formulates an end-to-end hybrid deep learning architecture combining the pixel-level spatial preservation of U-Net with the global self-attention mechanisms of Vision Transformers (ViT) for automated colorectal polyp segmentation, mitigating false negatives across irregular topological boundaries.

Abstract: Accurate colorectal polyp segmentation in colonoscopy sequences is paramount for early oncological prevention. Conventional CNN architectures often struggle with boundary ambiguities and heterogeneous polyp scales. This work integrates patch-based Vision Transformer encoding blocks into a symmetric U-Net skip-connection framework, effectively coupling local texture feature maps with long-range contextual relationships. Benchmark evaluations show pronounced enhancements in Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU).
Vision Transformer (ViT) U-Net PyTorch Semantic Segmentation Medical AI

Mediscan-HybridViT: Multi-Class Medical Image Classification Using a Hybrid CNN–Transformer, DenseNet-121, and Ensemble Decision Strategies
Prity Rani Das, et al.
Official Research Implementation • Multi-Class Medical Diagnostic Framework

Engineered a multi-class medical diagnostic classification architecture combining DenseNet-121 feature reuse networks with Transformer attention blocks under an ensemble decision fusion strategy to achieve high diagnostic sensitivity across heterogeneous pathology classes.

Abstract: Medical diagnostic image classification demands neural models that reconcile local fine-grained anatomical variations with global spatial organization. Mediscan-HybridViT combines DenseNet-121 convolutional backbones with multi-head self-attention Transformer layers, culminating in an ensemble decision policy that aggregates complementary feature representations to minimize misclassification across multi-class clinical imaging modalities.

Comparative Study of Computational Methods for Classifying 5 Types of Skin Diseases: Traditional Image Processing vs. Deep Learning
Prity Rani Das, et al.
Comparative Research Project • Dermatology Computational Diagnostics

Investigates diagnostic performance trade-offs between handcrafted image processing descriptors (GLCM, color histograms, SVM classifiers) versus modern deep convolutional transfer learning networks across 5 distinct dermatological conditions.

Abstract: Skin lesion diagnosis poses significant challenges due to subtle inter-class variations and intra-class phenotypic differences. This paper implements a comparative framework evaluating classical feature engineering against modern deep learning pipelines. We document quantitative evaluations across precision, recall, and ROC-AUC curves, delineating the practical clinical scenarios where lightweight feature extraction rivals deep neural networks.
Dermatology AI Transfer Learning Feature Engineering SVM vs. CNN

Visual and Geometric Feature Extraction from Leaf Images Using Contour and Morphological Analysis
Prity Rani Das
Computer Vision & Morphometrics Research Implementation

Algorithmic workflow combining adaptive thresholding, digital morphological filtering, and contour tracing to extract quantitative geometric invariants (eccentricity, convexity, aspect ratio, solidity) from botanical specimens.

Abstract: Quantitative morphometrics provides vital signals for taxonomic classification and plant disease identification. This project details an automated computational pipeline implemented in Python and OpenCV that standardizes geometric feature extraction from botanical leaf imagery with high mathematical fidelity.
