B.Sc. Undergraduate Thesis Institute of Information Technology, NSTU • Deep Learning & Medical Imaging

A Novel Hybrid Deep Learning Model Using U-Net and Vision Transformer for Polyp Segmentation in Medical Imaging

Principal Researcher: Prity Rani Das, et al.

Colorectal polyp segmentation is critical for early oncology intervention, yet conventional convolutional architectures struggle with irregular morphological boundaries, illumination artifacts, and varying polyp scales in colonoscopy frames. This thesis introduces a novel hybrid pipeline unifying patch-based Vision Transformer (ViT) self-attention with the multi-scale spatial decoding scaffold of U-Net to simultaneously capture micro-textures and global contextual dependencies.

Hybrid ViT + U-Net Architecture Coupled multi-head self-attention encoders with symmetric skip-connections to eliminate spatial resolution loss.
Benchmark Validated Demonstrated superior Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and Mean IoU over standard convolutional baseline architectures.
Full Thesis Abstract: Early detection of colorectal adenomas through colonoscopy substantially reduces patient mortality. However, manual endoscopic polyp delineation remains error-prone due to mucosal variations and edge blurring. This research introduces and validates a hybrid architecture fusing Vision Transformers (ViT) with U-Net. Transformer attention mechanisms model long-range spatial context, while the U-Net convolutional decoder preserves localized edge gradients. Experimental evaluations on benchmark medical imaging datasets confirm significant reductions in false positives and heightened precision along ambiguous tissue margins.
